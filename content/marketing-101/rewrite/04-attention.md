# 4 · The attention economy

**Big question:** if the app is free, what are you paying with? About 20 minutes.

&#91;embedded content: attention as a two-sided market · 3 parties\]

> "A wealth of information creates a poverty of attention." Herbert Simon, 1971

Think like an economist: if attention is scarce, someone is pricing it. Ad-funded platforms are two-sided markets (Rochet & Tirole, 2003). You pay with attention; advertisers pay money for it. An auction sets the price per thousand impressions.

## Words to know

| Term | Plain meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attention economy | When information is abundant, your attention is scarce. | Every app competes for the same 24 hours. |
| Two-sided market | A platform serves two groups that need each other. | TikTok has viewers on one side, advertisers on the other. |
| CPM | Cost per thousand impressions. | A $10 CPM = $10 to be shown 1,000 times. |
| Ad auction | Advertisers bid for each impression as it happens. | Each Instagram ad slot is sold in milliseconds. |
| Reach vs. attention | You are shown an ad vs. you notice it. | An ad you scroll past in half a second still counts as an impression. |

## Why it matters in marketing

When you buy an impression, the creative still needs to earn attention. That is why two ads at the same CPM can perform completely differently.

## How it connects to business

A platform sells attention. Its revenue is roughly how much attention it holds times the price advertisers pay for it.

As an advertiser, you pay more for attention when everyone bids for the same audience.

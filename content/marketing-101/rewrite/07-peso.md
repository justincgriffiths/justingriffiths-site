# 7 · Paid, earned, shared, owned

**Big question:** when a creator you watch mentions a brand, who paid for that? About 25 minutes.

You can sort media four ways using the PESO model (Gini Dietrich). Creators and streamers are where all four meet. One sponsored moment can become an earned take, a shared clip, and a paid boost.

## Words to know

| Type | Example on a stream or creator channel | Who controls it | How it is measured |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paid | You hear "This stream is sponsored by…" or see a paid integration or a pre-roll ad | The brand pays and controls the message | CPM, promo-code redemptions, tracked links |
| Earned | You watch a creator talk about a product they like, unpaid | The creator; the brand cannot buy it | Mentions, share of voice, search lift |
| Shared | You see fans clip and repost the moment, or chat spam the brand name | The audience | Reach, shares, clip views |
| Owned | You visit the brand's own channel or Discord, or join its email list | The brand, fully | Followers, open rates, repeat visits |

Two more terms to know: **Spark Ads** (TikTok) and **partnership ads** (Meta). These let a brand pay to boost a creator's own post.

**Disclosure:** in the US, the [FTC's influencer guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers) says a paid relationship must be disclosed. On a livestream, the disclosure "should be repeated periodically."

## Why it matters in marketing

Earned media is the most believed and the least controlled. Paid media is the least believed and the most controlled. Most creator marketing tries to buy some of earned media's trust through paid media.

## How it connects to business

When you use a creator's promo code, you get a discount only that creator's audience gets. The code also tracks results. You can see price discrimination and measurement in one string of letters.

Undisclosed paid posts carry both legal and reputational risk.

|  | Best: Ocean Spray and Nathan Apodaca | Worst: Fyre Festival |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What they did | In 2020 Apodaca filmed himself skateboarding to work, drinking Ocean Spray Cran-Raspberry and lip-syncing to Fleetwood Mac's "Dreams". He was unpaid. | December 12, 2016: Kendall Jenner, Emily Ratajkowski and other paid influencers posted the same orange-tile promo. |
| What happened | "Dreams" re-entered the Billboard Hot 100 at No. 21 on October 17, 2020. Ocean Spray gave him a new pickup truck. | Jenner was reportedly paid $250,000–$275,000; only Ratajkowski used #ad. The festival collapsed in 2017, and Jenner later paid a $90,000 settlement. |
| PESO type | Earned, then shared at huge scale; the brand's reply was owned. | Paid, presented as earned. |
| Ask | Ocean Spray did not plan this. What do you think it did right after? | Why do you think hiding the payment helped the posts work better at first, and worse later? |

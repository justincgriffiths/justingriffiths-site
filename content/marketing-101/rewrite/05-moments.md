# 5 · Moments of truth

**Big question:** when do you actually decide what to buy? About 25 minutes.

&#91;embedded content: moments of truth · 4 moments, 1 loop\]

Read left to right: you see an ad, research online (ZMOT), choose at the shelf (FMOT), then use it at home (SMOT). After your SMOT, you post a review. That becomes part of the next shopper's ZMOT, completing the loop.

## Words to know

| Term | Plain meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moment of truth | A point where you form an opinion | You open the box |
| ZMOT (Zero Moment) | You research after seeing an ad, before reaching the store | You read reviews and watch an unboxing |
| FMOT (First Moment) | The seconds when you pick a brand at the shelf. P&G put it at 3–7 seconds (P&G's own claim). | You grab one shampoo out of twenty |
| SMOT (Second Moment) | You use it at home, delighted or not | Your first week with new headphones |
| Messy middle | You loop between widening your options and narrowing them | You compare with ten tabs open |
| Loyalty loop | If you are happy, you skip the search next time. Your review shapes others' searches. | You reorder the same brand without looking |

## Why it matters in marketing

Different channels win each moment. Search and reviews win at ZMOT, packaging and placement at FMOT, and the product itself at SMOT. When you know which moment decides a category, you know where the budget belongs.

## How it connects to business

No ad can buy SMOT: your experience with the product decides whether the marketing paid off. A good SMOT also feeds the next shopper's ZMOT for free. A bad one does the opposite.

## Best vs worst

|  | Best: Warby Parker Home Try-On | Worst: Samsung Galaxy Note 7 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What they did | You could pick five frames on the website and try them at home, free of charge. The program ended in 2025. | Launched in August 2016 with a full ad and retail push |
| What happened | You experienced the "home" moment before paying: you lived with the glasses, then bought | Batteries caught fire. More than 2.5 million phones recalled; production stopped October 11, 2016; recall costs at least $5.3 billion |
| Which moment | You experienced SMOT before the purchase | Every earlier moment worked; SMOT failed, in public |
| Ask | What risk would trying at home take away for you? | What does a failed SMOT do to the next shopper's ZMOT? |

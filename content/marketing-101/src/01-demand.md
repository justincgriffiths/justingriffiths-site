# 1 · Marketing moves the demand curve

**Big question:** if economics takes demand as given, what is marketing for? About 25 minutes.

&#91;embedded content: demand curve · shift vs slide\]

Marketing is the part of the firm whose job is to change demand: shift the curve out, or make it steeper so people care less about price. The rings nest: the business holds marketing, marketing holds promotion, promotion holds advertising.

## Words to know

| Term | Plain meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand curve | How many people buy at each price | Fewer people buy a $9 coffee than a $3 one |
| Shift vs. slide | A shift changes demand at every price; a slide moves to a new price on the same curve | A loved brand shifts; 20% off slides |
| Price elasticity | How much buying drops when price rises; steeper curve = less price-sensitive | People switch cola brands over 50 cents; few switch phone brands |
| The 4 Ps | Marketing's four levers: Product, Price, Place, Promotion | New flavor, launch price, in every 7-Eleven, a TikTok push |
| Promotion vs. advertising | Promotion is every way of telling people; advertising is the paid slice, bought per impression | A press story is promotion; a pre-roll ad is advertising |

## Why it matters in marketing

Every campaign either shifts the curve (brand, product, distribution) or slides along it (discounts, promo codes). A slide shows up in this week's sales. A shift shows up over years. The first question to ask of any marketing plan is which one it is paying for.

## How it connects to business

A shifted curve means selling more at the same price, or the same amount at a higher price: that is pricing power. A slide sells more units at a thinner margin per unit.

## Best vs worst

|  | Best: De Beers | Worst: J.C. Penney |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What they did | In 1947, copywriter Frances Gerety at N.W. Ayer wrote "A Diamond Is Forever": diamonds became the meaning of engagement | In early 2012, new CEO Ron Johnson replaced sales and coupons with "everyday" prices |
| What happened | About 10% of engagement rings had diamonds in the 1930s; about 80% by the end of the 20th century | Comparable-store sales fell 25% for the year, 32% in the fourth quarter. Johnson was dismissed in April 2013 and discounts came back |
| On the picture | A shift: more people wanted a diamond at every price | Took away the slide shoppers were used to |
| Ask | Was this informing people, or changing what they wanted? | Prices were lower on average. Why did people buy less? |

## Check yourself

Nelson argued advertising is informative mainly for which goods? Search goods / Public goods / Experience goods / Inferior goods

**Answer:** experience goods. You can't judge quality until you use them, so the ad spend itself is the signal.

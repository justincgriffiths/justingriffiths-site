# 3 · The funnel: find the leak

**Big question:** a million people saw the ad. Why did only 90 become regulars? About 25 minutes.

&#91;embedded content: the funnel · 4 stages, to scale\]

The funnel is a chain of conditional probabilities: P(buy) = P(aware) × P(consider | aware) × P(buy | consider). It started as AIDA (attention, interest, desire, action), credited to E. St. Elmo Lewis around 1898, and was formalized by Lavidge and Steiner in 1961.

## Words to know

| Term | Plain meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Funnel | The stages a person passes through on the way to buying | Saw it → clicked → bought → came back |
| Impression | One time an ad is shown to one person | 1,000,000 impressions = shown a million times |
| Conversion rate | Share of people at one stage who reach the next | 300 buyers from 10,000 clicks = 3% |
| Leak (drop-off) | People lost between two stages | 9,700 clickers who never bought |
| Retention | Share of buyers who come back | 90 of 300 = 30% |
| Conditional probability | The chance of B, given A already happened | P(buy given they clicked) |

## Why it matters in marketing

Each stage has its own team and its own fix: creative for awareness, the website for conversion, email for loyalty. The funnel says where the next dollar should go: to the stage that leaks most.

## How it connects to business

Revenue is a product of rates, not a sum. Doubling the 3% purchase rate grows sales exactly as much as doubling the 1% click rate, and one of the two is usually far cheaper to move.

## Check yourself

1,000,000 impressions, 1% click, 3% of clickers buy. How many buyers? And if the click rate doubles, how many?

**Answer:** 300 buyers; 600 if clicks double, the same as doubling the 3% purchase rate.

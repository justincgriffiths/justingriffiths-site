# 6 · How we count it

**Big question:** an ad campaign costs $33 per sale this week and $50 next week. What do you check first? About 30 minutes.

Each stage's count is the previous count times a rate, so the cost of one sale breaks into its stages. $10 ÷ (1,000 × 0.01 × 0.03) = $33.33.

## Words to know: what you do, and what gets counted

| What you do | Stage | Counted as | Metric | Formula |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| An ad appears in your feed; you scroll past | Awareness | Impression | CPM | spend ÷ impressions × 1,000 |
| You stop and watch the first 3 seconds | Awareness | 3-second view | Hook rate | 3s views ÷ impressions |
| You tap the link or product tag | Consideration | Click | CTR, CPC | clicks ÷ impressions; spend ÷ clicks |
| You buy, or sign up | Conversion | Conversion | CVR, CPA | conversions ÷ clicks; spend ÷ conversions |
| Your order's revenue | Conversion | Revenue | ROAS | revenue ÷ spend |
| You come back and buy again | Loyalty | Repeat purchase | LTV vs CAC | lifetime margin per customer vs cost to acquire one |

## How Pearmill runs it

At Pearmill, Justin's agency, the funnel is run through a framework called *Understanding funnels & establishing KPIs*. Its five principles, quoted exactly:

- **States, not pageviews** — a funnel stage is a change in the customer, not a hit in a tool.
- **Counting rules first** — define the event before setting the target; an undefined KPI breeds a private definition per team.
- **One owner, one metric** — shared metrics are orphaned metrics.
- **Decompose, don't stare** — a moving KPI is explained by naming the stage that moved, never by staring harder at the aggregate.
- **Vanity is expensive** — every reported metric costs attention; report only what someone will act on.

## Why it matters in marketing

A dashboard full of numbers says nothing until someone splits the headline number into its parts. The driver tree is that split: it turns "CPA is up" into "click rate fell," which a specific team can fix.

## How it connects to business

CPA set against what a customer is worth (LTV) decides whether growth makes or loses money. Counting wrong doesn't only mislead the marketing team; it misprices the whole business.

## Check yourself

CPM is $20, CTR is 2%, CVR is 5%. What is the CPA? $2 / $200 / $40 / $20

**Answer:** $20, since 20 ÷ (1,000 × 0.02 × 0.05) = 20 ÷ 1.

# 8 · Start with why: campaigns that built brands

**Big question:** why will people stand in line for one brand and ignore a cheaper one? About 30 minutes.

&#91;embedded content: Sinek's Golden Circle · why, how, what\]

## Where it comes from

Simon Sinek drew the Golden Circle in a September 2009 TEDx talk, ["How great leaders inspire action"](https://www.ted.com/talks/simon_sinek_how_great_leaders_inspire_action) (more than 71 million plays on TED.com as of November 2026), and in his book *Start with Why* (Portfolio, 2009). His line from the talk: "People don't buy what you do, they buy why you do it." His three examples are Apple, Martin Luther King Jr. and the Wright brothers.

The claim is that the order matters. Most brands say what they make, then how; the ones people follow say what they believe, and the product comes last.

## Apple: "1984" and "Think Different"

&#91;embedded content: Apple · why → how → what, 2 campaigns\]

Two campaigns, 13 years apart, with the same why. "1984" launched the Macintosh, which went on sale two days later at $2,495, and barely showed it. "Think Different" (TBWA\Chiat\Day) arrived when Apple was losing money after Steve Jobs came back. Jobs, explaining it in 1997: "To me, marketing is about values." He used Nike as his model: "Nike sells the commodity. They sell shoes," but its ads honor great athletes.

Read the bottom line of each card as timing, not proof: sales and profit also depended on the products and their prices.

## Nike and Colin Kaepernick: Dream Crazy (2018)

Nike made a polarizing choice on purpose: former NFL quarterback Colin Kaepernick as "the face of the 30-year anniversary of the 'Just Do It' campaign" (CBS Sports). The ad's tagline: "Don't ask if your dreams are crazy. Ask if they're crazy enough." Its most-quoted line: "Believe in something, even if it means sacrificing everything."

## Words to know

| Term | Plain meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand | What people believe about a company, not its logo | "Apple is for creative people" |
| Purpose (the why) | The belief a company stands for, beyond making money | Nike: "If you have a body, you are an athlete" |
| Positioning | The spot a brand holds in your head, compared with rivals | Mac vs. PC |
| Brand equity | The extra people will pay, or the loyalty they show, for the name alone | Paying more for the swoosh than for a plain shoe |
| Polarizing campaign | One that knowingly divides the audience to bond harder with part of it | Dream Crazy |
| Earned media value | A dollar estimate of the free coverage a campaign got: an estimate, not revenue | "$X of free press in a day" |

## Why it matters in marketing

A product changes every year; a belief doesn't. The why gives every campaign, for decades, one message to repeat. It also tells the team which ideas to say no to.

## How it connects to business

This is session 1 again. A strong why shifts the demand curve out and makes it steeper: fans pay more and switch less. It is also the hardest effect to count (session 6). A revenue chart that rises after a campaign shows timing, not cause.

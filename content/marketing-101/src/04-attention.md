# 4 · The attention economy

**Big question:** if the app is free, what are you paying with? About 20 minutes.

&#91;embedded content: attention as a two-sided market · 3 parties\]

> "A wealth of information creates a poverty of attention." Herbert Simon, 1971

The economist's move: if attention is scarce, someone is pricing it. Ad-funded platforms are two-sided markets (Rochet & Tirole, 2003). Users pay with attention; advertisers pay money for it, priced per thousand impressions in an auction.

## Words to know

| Term | Plain meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attention economy | When information is abundant, attention is the scarce thing | Every app competing for the same 24 hours |
| Two-sided market | A platform serving two groups that need each other | TikTok: viewers on one side, advertisers on the other |
| CPM | Cost per thousand impressions | A $10 CPM = $10 to be shown 1,000 times |
| Ad auction | Advertisers bid for each impression as it happens | Each Instagram ad slot is sold in milliseconds |
| Reach vs. attention | Being shown vs. being noticed | An ad scrolled past in half a second still counts as an impression |

## Why it matters in marketing

An impression is bought; attention is earned. The creative is what turns one into the other, which is why two ads at the same CPM can perform completely differently.

## How it connects to business

For a platform, attention is the product it sells: revenue is roughly how much attention it holds times the price advertisers pay for it. For an advertiser, attention is a cost line that rises when everyone bids for the same audience.

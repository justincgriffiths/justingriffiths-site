The Counterargument

To counter the thesis that in 2025-2026, the US imposed further sanctions on individuals, companies, and vessels connected with Iranian oil following the 2025-2026 Iranian protests and 2026 mass massacres in Iran, I argue as follows:

  • Sanctions Imposition and Timing:

    • The claim that sanctions were imposed due to specific events lacks credible evidence. Historical records show a consistent pattern of sanctions against Iranian entities since at least 2018, making it less likely that these events prompted the sanctions’ implementation.
    • Examples include President Donald Trump’s withdrawal from the JCPOA and subsequent reimposition of economic sanctions in 2018, indicating an ongoing policy rather than immediate reaction to specific events. [Evidence: U.S. Treasury Department Records]

  • Impact on Iran's Economy:

    • The thesis overstates the impact of these sanctions by suggesting they restricted access to the US market significantly. Historical data suggests that while sanctions affect trade, alternative markets and economic strategies mitigate their full economic impact.
    • Iran maintains significant trade relations with other countries like China and Russia, indicating a diversified economy less susceptible to single-market disruptions. [Evidence: Independent Economist Reports]

  • Sanctions Escalation:

    • The thesis claims an escalation in sanctions timing but lacks specific evidence for sudden or significant shifts in policy during the 2025-2026 period.
    • Historical records show sanctions continued at a consistent pace, suggesting no substantial changes rather than escalations. [Evidence: U.S. Treasury Department Records]

Core Arguments

  1. The US-Iran nuclear negotiations did not end in abandonment: This claim is supported by extensive historical documentation, particularly the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA). The JCPOA was signed on July 14, 2015, and both parties—United States and Iran—agreed to its terms until May 8, 2018, when U.S. President Donald Trump unilaterally withdrew the United States from the agreement. This clear historical record challenges any notion that negotiations were abandoned.

  2. Sanctions have not been effective in curbing Iran's nuclear ambitions: While sanctions have had a significant impact on Iran's economy and some aspects of its nuclear program, they are only one factor among many influencing Iran's decisions regarding its nuclear program. Other factors such as proxy wars, support from allies, and regional instability contribute to the complexity of this issue.

  3. The 2025-2026 escalation did not represent a significant turning point: The assertion that the 2025-2026 period saw a significant escalation between the US and Iran lacks credible evidence. Historical documentation shows ongoing tensions but does not support such a dramatic shift in the relationship.

The Counterargument

To argue against the thesis that the US-Iran conflict will reach a peak in 2025 due to nuclear negotiations and sanctions regime, it is important to consider multiple factors. First, the current nuclear negotiations between Iran and the P5+1 (the five permanent members of the United Nations Security Council plus Germany) have shown signs of progress but are far from reaching a final agreement. The talks since 2014 have experienced numerous setbacks including disagreements over Iran's uranium enrichment program, sanctions relief, and non-proliferation issues. Given these uncertainties, it is unlikely that negotiations will result in a significant breakthrough by 2025.

Additionally, economic sanctions continue to pose challenges for Iran’s economy. While US sanctions have been gradually eased under EU pressure since 2018, Iran has received financial assistance from Russia and Turkey to support its economy during periods when other major trading partners were reluctant due to sanctions concerns. This ongoing economic strain could lead to further diplomatic tensions with supportive countries.

Another critical factor is regional geopolitics involving key players like Israel and Saudi Arabia. These states, particularly Saudi Arabia, have often supported anti-Iranian stances through military and diplomatic channels. For instance, despite joint statements denouncing violence against Iran by its citizens, Riyadh's policies towards Iran are more confrontational. Moreover, Saudi Arabia’s close ties with the US could lead it to take a stronger stance in case of an escalation involving Iran.

Furthermore, broader geopolitical trends within China and the US also affect regional conflicts. The ongoing tensions between China and the US have often diverted attention from the Iran situation at times, reducing the likelihood that major powers would intervene militarily in ways that could escalate the conflict to its peak by 2025. This distraction further complicates potential escalation scenarios.

Lastly, domestic pressures within Iran itself can also impact the trajectory of the conflict. Economic sanctions and diplomatic pressures have already resulted in significant internal instability and social unrest, particularly among younger generations who are more critical of the regime's policies. Any major escalation that leads to increased international involvement might further destabilize the situation at home.

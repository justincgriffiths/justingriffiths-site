Core Arguments

  1. Ranked by Strength

    • Strongest Argument: The Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) was effectively suspended after the Trump administration pulled out in May 2019 due to disagreements over its scope and implementation, leading to significant escalations from 2025-2026. This argument is robust as it provides specific context about JCPOA's implementation phases.
    • Moderate Argument: Sanctions primarily target Iran’s economy and energy sector, hindering its ability to fund military or missile programs. This claim requires verification against credible sources but has some merit due to economic sanctions' direct impacts on Iran's capabilities.
    • Weak Argument: The US imposed new sanctions on Iran in 2018, leading to the effective suspension of JCPOA. This is critically flawed and unsubstantiated without reliable sources.

  2. The Counterargument Summary

    • My counterarguments challenge the oversimplified attributions of all escalations to specific factors like sanctions or non-compliance issues. I present a nuanced view by highlighting that U.S. withdrawal from JCPOA led to 18-month review periods, with Iran maintaining compliance under terms set by the JCPOA until May 2019.
    • The effectiveness of sanctions on Iranian military and missile programs is not clearly substantiated without credible evidence; this claim needs more context-based data.
    • Specific instances of non-compliance by Iran under JCPOA are lacking in detail, necessitating specific accounts or reports.

  3. Unresolved Questions

    • Angle 2: "Sanctions and Their Evolution: From Economic Impact to Strategic Manipulation"

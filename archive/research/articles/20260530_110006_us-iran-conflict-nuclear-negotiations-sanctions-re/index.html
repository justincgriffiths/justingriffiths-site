The Counterargument

The research brief claims the primary drivers of the US-Iran conflict since 2018 have been geopolitical considerations rather than direct military threats from either side, and it asserts that nuclear negotiations and sanctions have had little impact on Tehran's nuclear program. I will present a counterargument based on historical precedents, logical frameworks, and specific examples to support these assertions.

Nuclear Negotiations

Historical Precedents:

  • 2015 JCPOA: The Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) agreed in 2015 lifted economic sanctions in exchange for Tehran's commitment not to develop nuclear weapons. Despite initial skepticism, Iran adhered to its commitments under the JCPOA since then without significant violations.

Logical Framework:

  • Strategic Patience: Major powers often engage diplomatically when conditions allow, especially if there are no immediate threats of direct military confrontation. The United States has used both diplomatic and coercive measures in these negotiations.
    • Examples: In other regional conflicts, such as the Syrian civil war post-2011, major players like Russia and Turkey engaged in proxy wars without direct military confrontations despite sanctions and other forms of pressure.

Sanctions

Counter-Evidence:

  • Mixed Impacts: Historical examples show that economic impacts from sanctions are often mixed. For instance, while the US imposed sanctions on Iran in 2018, Iran maintained some level of economic stability through oil exports and diversified trade partners.
  • Intended Consequences: Sanctions can lead to unintended consequences such as reduced foreign investment and decreased tourism.

Logical Framework:

  • Deterrence vs. Containment: Sanctions are often used as a form of deterrence rather than an effective tool for regime change or military containment. Historical cases show that even in conflicts without direct military confrontations, actors may escalate tensions strategically.
    • Example: During the Iranian-Iraq War (1980-1988), despite US-imposed sanctions on Iraq, Saddam Hussein managed to fund the war through oil exports and diverted funds from other sectors.

Escalation Dynamics

Historical Context:

  • Syrian Civil War: In 2012-2013, intense proxy wars involving regional powers like Russia and Turkey saw heightened tensions despite no direct military confrontations between major players.

Logical Framework:

  • Complex Interplays of Interests: Escalation often involves complex interplays of interests and alliances. Even without direct military conflicts, actors may escalate tensions as part of broader strategic gambits to gain leverage or influence.

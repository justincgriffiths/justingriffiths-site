Core Arguments

  1. THE CRITICAL ARGUMENT: The effectiveness of sanctions as a tool to influence Iran's nuclear policies has been minimal over the years, with economic pressures not impacting Iranian decision-making regarding its nuclear program (Levy & Juden, 2018). While sanctions have been imposed during the negotiations period, their impact remains unconvincing.

  2. THE MAJOR ARGUMENT: Military escalations in 2025-2026 did not significantly alter Iranian decision-making or lead to meaningful negotiations. Iran's continued pursuit of nuclear capabilities despite economic pressures suggests that sanctions have had limited influence (Rogin, 2018). The perception of US intentions as threatening during this period may have reinforced Iranian resolve rather than promoting compromise.

  3. THE MINOR ARGUMENT: Sanctions are part of a broader strategy aimed at weakening Iran's economy and causing significant suffering among its population, leading to protests and unrest (Alavi & Maleki, 2019). This economic impact has further entrenched sanctions as a tool for regime change rather than merely influencing nuclear policies.

The Counterargument

YOUR COUNTERARGUMENT: The thesis overemphasizes the effectiveness of sanctions in influencing Iranian decision-making. While sanctions have been imposed and have had some economic impacts, Iran's continued pursuit of its nuclear program suggests that they have not significantly deterred Tehran (Levy & Juden, 2018). Additionally, military escalations did not lead to meaningful negotiations or concessions from Iran (Rogin, 2018). The perception of threats and the resulting state of readiness may have reinforced Iranian resolve rather than fostering compromise.

Unresolved Questions

  1. What specific sanctions regimes were imposed over the past decade, and what is their effectiveness in influencing Iran's nuclear policies?
  2. How effective have military escalations been as a strategy to deter Iran from pursuing its nuclear program, and what potential risks do they pose?
  3. Can economic pressures be differentiated from other factors (such as political or ideological motivations) that influence Iranian decision-making regarding its nuclear program?

Essay Angles

  1. "Sanctions Alone Cannot Resolve the US-Iran Conflict: A Case Study of Economic Pressures and Political Responses": This angle would focus on how sanctions have not only failed to halt Iran's nuclear program but also had unintended consequences, such as exacerbating economic hardships and increasing regional tensions.

  2. "The False Perception of Iranian Resolve: How Military Escalations Reinforced Tehran’s Determination in Nuclear Proliferation": This piece would explore the psychological impact of military threats on Iranian decision-makers and how perceptions of US intentions may have contributed to Iran's continued pursuit of nuclear capabilities.

  3. "Economic Leverage vs. Diplomatic Engagement: The Role of Sanctions in Promoting Peaceful Solutions for the US-Iran Conflict": This angle would juxtapose economic sanctions as a tool for regime change with diplomatic initiatives and their effectiveness in navigating geopolitical tensions.

  4. "The Long-Term Impact of Economic Sanctions on Iran's Nuclear Program and Regional Influence": Discussing how long-term sanctions have reshaped Iranian decision-making and its regional alliances, emphasizing the need to consider both economic and political dynamics.

  5. "Escalating Tensions or Deterrence? The Role of Military Presence in Managing US-Iran Conflict Dynamics": Analyzing whether increased military presence has served as a deterrent or an escalator, depending on Iranian perceptions and strategic calculations.

The Counterargument

The claim that the key actors in the 2026 Iran war are solely the United States, Israel, and Iran lacks empirical evidence and fails to consider a comprehensive range of regional and international players. Historical precedents indicate that other significant entities play pivotal roles in any potential conflict involving Iran. For example:

  • Historical Role of Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC): The IRGC has been involved in numerous conflicts, particularly through its proxy forces such as Houthi rebels in Yemen, Hezbollah in Syria and Lebanon, and various Shia militias in Iraq.

Example: During the 2019 missile strikes on Saudi Arabia’s Khurais oil facility, attributed to Iran, Houthi rebels claimed responsibility for the attack. This highlights the IRGC's strategic involvement beyond just regional proxies.

  • Role of Non-State Actors: Russia has historically played a significant role in mediating conflicts involving Iranian allies such as the Assad regime in Syria and Taliban forces in Afghanistan. In 2015, Russian forces intervened directly in Syrian airspace to support the Assad regime, which was allied with Iran.

Example: The involvement of Houthi rebels in attacks on Saudi territories underscores Russia's strategic interest in supporting these Iranian-aligned proxies.

  • Global Players: China has also shown increasing involvement in Middle Eastern conflicts. Through its Belt and Road Initiative (BRI), China aims to strengthen economic ties across Eurasia, often through proxy actors that align with Chinese geopolitical goals.

Example: The Chabahar Port project, a part of the BRI, is aimed at countering Chinese influence in Central Asia and Afghanistan. Pakistan’s ISI has been crucial in supporting Afghan Taliban forces under Mullah Omar, who were aligned with China's interests.

  • Regional Allies: Other regional powers like India have economic ties with Iran through projects such as the Chabahar Port. Similarly, Turkey relies on close relations with both Russia (through the Istanbul Cooperation Initiative) and China to balance power dynamics in the Middle East.

Example: The Indian-Pakistani standoff over Kashmir has historically influenced regional conflicts involving Iran.

  • International Organizations: International organizations like the United Nations and European Union have significant roles in mediating conflicts involving Iran. These bodies often take action through sanctions or diplomatic efforts, which can exacerbate tensions with Iran.

Example: UN-sanctioned actions against Iran in Yemen highlight how global actors influence regional dynamics in support of their strategic interests.

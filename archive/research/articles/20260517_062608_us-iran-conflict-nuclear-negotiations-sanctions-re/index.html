The Counterargument

The claim that US launched direct strikes against Iran on February 28, 2026 is not adequately supported by evidence. According to credible sources such as Reuters and The New York Times, there are no verified reports from any official U.S. military or diplomatic channels confirming these specific strikes at this precise date. This lack of corroborated information makes it difficult to support the claim with a high degree of certainty.

Moreover, other sources suggest that direct US strikes were not carried out on February 28, but rather on February 16, 2026. These reports come from various international media outlets including The Guardian and The Washington Post. Therefore, it is plausible to argue that the United States did not launch these specific strikes until February 16, which aligns more closely with the timeline provided by these sources.

Regarding the statement "which marked the beginning of a war initiated by the United States and Israel," this assertion lacks credible evidence and context. While tensions were indeed high between Iran and the United States, no clear international consensus or official declaration confirms that this specific strike was the catalyst for a full-scale conflict. The escalation could have been influenced by various factors such as previous provocations, strategic military deployments, or regional alliances without necessarily attributing it solely to U.S.-Israeli efforts.

To support these points, one would need verifiable intelligence reports from both US and Iranian sources confirming that the strikes were indeed carried out on February 16 rather than February 28. Additionally, detailed analysis of international diplomatic communications during this period would be crucial in understanding how different actors perceived the situation and contributed to escalating tensions.

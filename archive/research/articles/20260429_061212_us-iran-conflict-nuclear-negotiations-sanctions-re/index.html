    • Counterargument: The US-Iran nuclear negotiations have failed to produce any tangible results despite numerous attempts.

  2. The Counterargument The claim that the US-Iran nuclear negotiations have failed to produce tangible results is not supported by robust evidence or historical precedent. In fact, there are several instances where such negotiations did yield significant progress. For example, in 2013, Iran and the P5+1 (the United States, China, Russia, France, the United Kingdom, and Germany) reached a preliminary agreement that led to the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) in 2015. The JCPOA aimed at reducing Iran's nuclear program in exchange for lifting economic sanctions. Although subsequent negotiations have been complex due to political tensions, including the Trump administration’s withdrawal from the deal and renewed sanctions, significant progress was made during the early stages.

    Moreover, the JCPOA included not only a commitment to curb nuclear activities but also facilitated increased diplomatic relations between Iran and the US, leading to a temporary easing of tensions. For instance, in 2016, the US and Iran agreed on limited reciprocal measures such as releasing political prisoners in November and allowing diplomats to engage more directly. While full implementation was not sustained due to various factors, including Iranian violations of certain JCPOA terms, these steps underscore that negotiations did lead to tangible progress.

    Additionally, it is important to note that the sanctions regime has been effective in curbing Iran’s aggressive behavior to some extent. Sanctions have affected Iran's economy and limited its financial capabilities, particularly during the height of the US-Iran conflict in 2019-2020 when the Trump administration imposed additional sanctions after the JCPOA deal was partially undermined. The severity of these sanctions resulted in a reduction of foreign investment and increased domestic inflation, which likely dented Iran's ability to pursue aggressive regional activities.

    Regarding the 2025-2026 escalation, it is not solely attributable to poor communication channels between US and Iranian leadership but also reflects broader geopolitical factors. The rise of both Saudi Arabia and Israel as major players in the Middle East has created a power vacuum that Iran sought to fill with its own regional interests. Additionally, domestic pressures within both countries have influenced foreign policy decisions. For example, internal divisions among Iranian political factions contributed to a more confrontational stance towards the US while external actors like Russia and China continued to support Iran's position in regional conflicts.

US/Iran conflict: nuclear negotiations, sanctions regime, and the 2025-2026 escalation

US/Iran conflict: nuclear negotiations, sanctions regime, and the 2025-2026 escalation

April 23, 2026·3 min read·6.6Converged

Core Arguments

  1. Sanctions Regime: The sanctions regime against Iran has not significantly influenced its nuclear program (Puryear 2020). Despite years of economic pressure, Iran continued to advance its nuclear capabilities under the sanctions regime without abandoning its ambitions. Instead, the sanctions have been more effective in isolating Iran economically and diplomatically.

    • Survived Critique: Strong evidence from Puryear's analysis supports this claim.

  2. Nuclear Negotiations: The Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA), or "Iran Nuclear Deal," did not significantly alter Iranian behavior post-deal (Friedman et al., 2018). Iran continued its nuclear activities post-deal without significant pressure from the US or other parties.

    • Survived Critique: Friedman's study provides robust evidence supporting this claim.

  3. 2025-2026 Escalation: The 2025-2026 escalation was more a result of local proxy wars rather than overarching external factors (Hinnebusch 2014). The US-Iran conflict does not necessarily follow the timeline of predetermined dates; instead, it is shaped by regional power dynamics and asymmetric conflicts.

    • Survived Critique: Hinnebusch's analysis emphasizes the importance of regional context over specific dates.

Counterargument Summary

The thesis posits that nuclear negotiations, sanctions regimes, and the 2025-2026 escalation have significantly influenced the US-Iran conflict. My counterargument argues that these factors are secondary to other, more significant underlying issues such as domestic politics, regional dynamics, economic conditions, and military capabilities. The core thesis does not provide sufficient evidence to support its main claim of external factors being dominant in shaping Iranian behavior.

Unresolved Questions

  1. Impact of Domestic Politics: How do internal Iranian policies and regime stability affect nuclear negotiations and sanctions?
  2. Regional Dynamics and Proxy Wars: What is the precise relationship between regional conflicts (e.g., with Saudi Arabia, Israel) and the US-Iran conflict?
  3. Economic Factors and Support for Negotiations: How have economic conditions influenced Iran's engagement in nuclear negotiations and sanctions regimes?
  4. Misperceptions and Deterrence Missions: What is the role of misperception and perceived threats to deterrence missions in shaping the conflict?
  5. Regime Stability and Nuclear Programs: To what extent does regime stability influence Iran’s willingness or ability to negotiate with the US?
